## What cig should carry away

**Test layers stage C needs before its first program runs, in order of what each catches:**

1. **The verifier on every pass.** cig already has this (`cig-script/src/lower/cir/verify.rs`'s `V1` to `V11` and `T1` to `T4`), and it catches a pass that drops, reorders, clones or hoists an effect, a step mark or a raising operation. First layer, and cheapest.
2. **A broken-twin test for every pass.** cig has this for the four current passes (`cig-script/src/lower/cir/pass/broken.rs`: `HoistBurnOp`, `SwapBurnOps`, and more). Adding a pass without adding a twin breaks `each_pass_has_a_negative_test_that_is_caught`. Second layer.
3. **A CIR interpreter run before and after each pass.** Not present today (entry 11). This is the layer that catches a pass that honors every verifier rule but still computes a different value, which the structural rules cannot see.
4. **Per-term specs on `Kind`.** Not present today (entry 13). This is the layer that lets a reader of the pass say what the pass must not change, without reading the pass; and the surface a VeriISLE-style SMT check lands on in the future.
5. **A random CIR generator plus the first three layers.** Not present today (entry 10). This is the layer that exercises combinations no hand-written test holds.
6. **A random source generator (cig-smith) plus the differential suite across engines.** Partially present (`cig-compile/tests/differential.rs`, `cig-compile/tests/differential/gen.rs`; the cig-compile#39 comment of 2026-11-08 says "213 the same, 0 different" when the suite was run on cig-script v1.18.0). Layer 6 catches a parse bug or a lowering bug that CIR-level testing cannot.
7. **A reducer that produces a human-readable minimal reproducer.** Partially present (`cig-compile/src/reduce.rs`'s `ddmin`). Layer 7 is for the operator: when the previous six find a bug, this makes it a report.
8. **A bisect over the pass list.** Not present today (entry 16; `--passes` flag exists, the driver does not). Layer 8 answers "which pass".
9. **The register allocator's symbolic checker on every stage C build.** Not present today, arrives with Cranelift (entry 14). Layer 9 catches a spill/reload/move that drops a value.
10. **DDC on stage 1's binary.** Not present today, arrives with self-hosting (entry 17). Layer 10 catches a planted stage 0.

**Carry-away findings, in priority order:**

- **A random-program generator is the cheapest way to raise what the differential suite covers.** cig-compile today runs cases from `examples/`, `tests/scripts/`, `tests/corpus/cig-orchestrator/` and the docs' `cig` fences, plus seeded line programs it shrinks by dropping statements (`cig-compile/tests/differential.rs`: `all_cases`, `random_case`, `shrink`); the cig-compile#39 comment of 2026-11-08 reports "213 the same, 0 different" on cig-script v1.18.0. 0 differences is a statement about what has been written, not what the compiler has not been asked (entry 1, entry 4).
- **cig has no undefined behavior, so a cig-smith generator does not need Csmith's effect tracking or points-to facts to pick safe expressions.** What it needs instead is a tracker that every burn op the generator emits has a matching compensation, that every loop has a bound that keeps the step budget inside `E515`, and that every map and list it builds remembers its insertion order so the checksum it exhales on exit is the same on every engine (entries 1, 2, 4).
- **A shrinker needs the fixed-point loop with a parallel candidate pool and more than one axis of removal.** Today `cigcompile reduce`'s `ddmin` (`cig-compile/src/reduce.rs:229`) drops whole lines, serially. C-Vise runs 20 pass kinds (lines, balanced-brace deletion, peephole, integer rewrites, syntax-aware cuts) and a worker per candidate; the paper that defined its design (Regehr et al. 2012, abstract and introduction) reports its best reducer produces outputs "more than 25 times smaller on average" than line-based delta debugging. The jump in reduction is the difference between an actionable bug report and one nobody will open (entries 5, 6, 7).
- **Cranelift pins its code generator on a validator of its own IR, not just on the source oracle.** `cranelift-interpreter`'s `Interpreter` runs a CLIF function the same code generator is about to compile; the fuzz target feeds a random valid CLIF function through both and compares (`cranelift-fuzzgen.rs`). cig's tree-walker runs source; for stage C's generator a CIR interpreter that runs module bytes would give an oracle the generator can be pinned to without producing a native binary first (entries 8, 9, 10).
- **Verified lowering rules buy more than checked rules; the register allocator can be checked in full.** VeriISLE proves ISLE expansions against an authoritative ISA specification using SMT and has reproduced three known bugs and found two unknown ones (Crocus, VanHattum et al. 2024); `regalloc2`'s symbolic checker (`regalloc2/src/checker.rs`) proves a produced allocation preserves the dataflow, which is a full proof of the output on every input the fuzzer makes. Stage C should start with the allocator's checker armed on every build (entry 13, entry 14).
- **Trusting a self-hosted cig means two independent compilers compiling it, not one compiler compiling itself.** Wheeler's diverse double-compiling (DDC) uses a second compiler that only has to be correct enough on the small set of language features the compiler uses, and shows the resulting binaries are identical when the two paths converge; cig's three engines (tree-walker, VM, stage C) are three independent paths over the same CIR, which is exactly what DDC needs (entry 17).
- **Alive2-style per-pass translation validation is deliberately out of this study.** The brief says "Another study covers Alive2 (translation validation of LLVM's passes): leave it". What this study carries is the complement: the verifier's structural rules plus a CIR interpreter are the lightweight substitute cig can land before Alive2-style equivalence arrives. Entry 11 is the interpreter; entry 13 is the specs the equivalence check would compare against.
- **EMI (Le et al. 2014) is the model for metamorphic variants, and cig has three today.** `cig-compile/tests/metamorphic.rs` runs three transforms (`renamed`, `split_constants`, `with_dead_code`), and the Le paper is the framing: an equivalent program variant must produce the same result at the same compiler level, so the compiler is validated against itself, not against a second oracle. EMI's Orion tool evaluated one transform: "pruning unexecuted code" (`papers/compilers/le-2014-emi.pdf`, section 1 "Compiler Bug Hunter: Orion" and section 3), reporting 147 confirmed bugs on GCC and LLVM in eleven months. The transform cig is still missing is pruning: given a run of a program over inputs, drop statements the run did not reach and compile both; the two must agree. Statement reorder where independent is a separate idea the EMI paper mentions as a possibility but does not evaluate.
- **The bisect driver is the smallest win per hour of implementation.** `cig-compile/src/main.rs:262` already takes `--passes a,b,c`; a bisect driver is a binary search on the prefix of the pass list and a comparator against `cig`, perhaps 150 lines in Rust, that reuses `cig-compile/src/reduce.rs`'s `Scratch` and `Tester`. #33's budget "under 30 s for a 1,000-line script" is met on the current four-pass list in at most four `cig-compile` invocations (entry 16).
- **The pass accounting from C-Vise is worth copying directly.** `cvise/utils/statistics.py`'s `PassStatistic` tracks `total_seconds`, `worked`, `failed` per pass across a run; printing this at the end of `cigcompile reduce` tells the operator which pass did the work and which was wasted budget. cig has nothing like it today (entry 6).
- **Cranelift's filetests set is the smallest set of test shapes to copy.** Four kinds cover almost every surface: `test_run` for value pinning, `test_compile` for machine-code pinning, `test_optimize` for IR pinning, `test_interpret` for oracle comparison. cig has one and a half (CIR-print golden, differential comparison) and can land the other two with one `.cig`/`.out` helper per kind (entry 12).
- **Hash-seed and clock stubs are cheap (#39's "Determinism checks") and catch the "accidentally non-deterministic" bugs.** cig's maps are string-keyed and iterated in insertion order (rule 4), so hashing is not observable today; a test that runs the corpus under a shuffled seed proves that is still true, which is a cheap guard against a hash-seed leak sneaking into a pass or a builtin.
- **The runtime checks (Miri, ASan, TSan) belong in cig-script, not cig-compile.** #39's comment classifies these as "belongs in cig-script"; this study agrees. They are a complement to, not a replacement for, differential fuzzing: ASan catches a double-free before the differential suite notices a wrong exhale.
- **A planted miscompile is the test every other layer is for.** `cig-compile/tests/differential.rs`'s `_a_planted_miscompile_is_found_by_the_pr_fuzz` (planned per #39's Tests table) should land with the first `cig-smith` build: a hand-written broken pass on a seeded corpus, caught in 2,000 programs. Without it, every other layer's budget is a guess.

## Design points

### 1. Csmith's effects: ruling out order-sensitive expressions at generation time

- **What it does, and why.** Each expression and statement in Csmith carries an `Effect` (the `Effect` class in `src/Effect.h`, with `read_var`, `write_var`, `is_read`, `is_written`, and its member vectors `read_vars`, `write_vars`, `lhs_write_vars`). When Csmith makes an `a + b` it records the two `read_var`s, and the generator refuses any sibling expression that writes what another reads, at a point where the C standard does not sequence the two. The result is that the generator never writes an expression whose value depends on an unspecified evaluation order. The Yang et al. 2011 paper says a generated program must not fall into any of "191 kinds of undefined behavior, nor depend on any of the 52 kinds of unspecified behavior, that are described in the C99 standard" (`papers/compilers/yang-2011-csmith.pdf` section 1).
- **For cig: adapt.** cig's language has no unspecified evaluation order: CIR's `Binary`, `Short`, `Index`, `SetIndex`, `Store` and `Call*` kinds each define their argument order (`cig-script/src/lower/cir/ir.rs`: `Kind`). What cig-smith does need to track in the same shape is **effect regions**: a `burn.op` is lexically nested in its burn and cannot be sibling to a top-level statement; an `exhale` is a `Step` and goes only with its whole block; a `try`'s catch body runs only when the body raised. A generator that tracked a per-node "what is inside my burn" and a per-node "what is inside my try" would never emit a `burn.op` outside a burn, which today the lowering would catch but the test-case author has to re-check for every program.
- **Decided by rule 1 (burns never move):** the kernel the generator writes burns through must be the one in `cig-script/src/burn/mod.rs`, so the generator's own effect tracker is a map from the CIR node it is building to a region id, not a `read_var`/`write_var` set. The second cig-specific hazard is the step budget: every loop and statement has a weight, so the generator pairs a loop bound with a budget ceiling it will not exceed. The third is the journal: a burn that writes needs a matching compensation, so the generator pairs a `BurnOp::write` with a `BurnOp::rm` under the same `Burn` node and hands both to the kernel in one call.
- **What would decide it.** `Decided by rule 1.`
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#39; decision (d).
- **Read.** `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/Effect.h` (`Effect`, `read_var`, `write_var`, `is_read`, `read_vars`, `write_vars`); `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/Effect.cpp`; `papers/compilers/yang-2011-csmith.pdf`; `cig-script/src/lower/cir/ir.rs` (`Kind`, `Burn`, `BurnOp`, `Step`); `cig-script/src/burn/mod.rs`.

### 2. Csmith's points-to facts: proving a dereference valid before emitting it

- **What it does, and why.** `src/FactPointTo.cpp` carries one fact per pointer, a set of variables it may point to, plus the special values `null`, `garbage` and `tbd` (`doc/pa.txt` section 1). `src/FactMgr.cpp` (`FactMgr`, `validate_fact`, `validate_assign`, `set_fact_in`, `set_fact_out`) joins facts at merge points and propagates them through statements; `src/Fact.h` (`Fact`) is the base. Every dereference the generator considers is checked against the fact of its pointer before the statement is emitted, so no program it prints reads through a null or dangling pointer. The paper's section 2 is explicit that this is the alternative to a safety wrapper at run time: Csmith does static safety at generation, so the generated program itself needs no sanitizer passes.
- **For cig: not applicable.** cig has no raw pointers, no addresses, no undefined dereference; a wrong index raises `E505` at run time, a wrong key raises `E506`, both at a span the generator already carries. The cost of this analysis in Csmith is a per-statement environment of facts: a `FactVec` in and out of every statement (`FactMgr::set_fact_in`, `set_fact_out`). cig-smith pays nothing comparable because the language removes the hazard. **Decided by rule 5:** the tree-walker is the oracle, and a wrong index on cig raises the same error at the same span on every engine, so a generated program whose index is out of range is a legitimate case, not an invalid one to filter. The one place a weaker analog is needed is name resolution: a generator that writes a call to a function before the function's declaration triggers `LowerError::Refused` (`cig-script/src/lower/cir/names.rs`), which is not a miscompile; the generator should emit declarations in a topological order to avoid the detour.
- **What would decide it.** `Decided by rule 5.`
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#39.
- **Read.** `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/FactMgr.h` (`FactMgr`, `validate_fact`, `validate_assign`, `set_fact_in`, `set_fact_out`); `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/FactPointTo.h`; `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/FactPointTo.cpp`; `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/Fact.h` (`Fact`); `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/doc/pa.txt`; `papers/compilers/yang-2011-csmith.pdf`; `cig-script/src/lower/cir/names.rs`.

### 3. YARPGen's generation-time evaluation: no safety wrappers, every value known

- **What it does, and why.** YARPGen does not wrap operations: instead the generator holds the value of every variable at every program point and, when an expression's evaluation at generation time would produce an undefined behavior, **rewrites the expression** before emitting it (`src/expr.cpp`: `AssignExpr::propagate_type`, `AssignExpr::propagate_value`, `UnaryExpr::rebuild`, `UnaryExpr::propagate_type`, `UnaryExpr::propagate_value`, `TypeCastExpr::propagate_type`, `TypeCastExpr::propagate_value`). The abstract-value computation returns a `UB` tag; a non-`NoUB` tag triggers `rebuild`, which replaces the offending operator or constant. The README (`README.rst` section 1) says the resulting program is "statically and dynamically correct C/C++ ... This implies no undefined behavior". The Livinskii et al. 2020 paper calls this the YARPGen contribution.
- **For cig: adapt.** cig's generator should know every value at every point, so each generated expression's result is the oracle for the checksum it prints. cig has no UB, but it has **errors that must happen at the same place**: `E503` on integer overflow, `E505` on a bad index, `E506` on a missing key (rule 2). The generator should choose, per expression, whether this one is a "finishes" program or a "raises at line N" program, and track the two outcomes exactly. A `propagate_value` that returned an `enum CigOutcome { Finishes, Raises(Code, Span) }` would give the differential suite a second oracle beside the tree-walker: the generator's own promise about where the error should land.
- **Contrasted with Csmith.** Csmith's approach is to wrap every operation (`runtime/safe_math_macros.m4`, `runtime/safe_abbrev.h`) so overflow is detected at run time and reported to a harness; YARPGen is better for cig because cig does not want a wrapper layer in generated programs. **Decided by rule 2.**
- **What would decide it.** `Decided by rule 2.`
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#39, cig-compile#33; decision (d).
- **Read.** `yarpgen-v1.1/src/expr.cpp` (`propagate_type`, `propagate_value`, `rebuild`); `yarpgen-v1.1/src/expr.h`; `yarpgen-v1.1/README.rst`; `papers/compilers/livinskii-2020-yarpgen.pdf`; `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/runtime/safe_math_macros.m4`; `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/runtime/safe_abbrev.h`; `cig-script/src/lower/cir/ir.rs` (`Binary`, `Short`, `Throw`).

### 4. Generation policies: biasing random programs toward the optimizer

- **What it does, and why.** YARPGen's `src/gen_policy.h` has `Pattern` and `ArithSSP` structs: the generator chooses a leaf class with `ArithLeafID` (`Data`, `Unary`, `Binary`, `TypeCast`, `CSE`, `MAX_LEAF_ID`) and a const-use pattern with `ArithSSP::ConstUse` (`CONST_BRANCH`, `HALF_CONST`) that skews the tree toward all-constant leaves or half-constant leaves, and a similar-operator pattern with `ArithSSP::SimilarOp` (`ADDITIVE`, `BITWISE`, `LOGIC`, `MUL`, `BIT_SH`, `ADD_MUL`) that keeps one operator family within an expression. These are the patterns the paper (`papers/compilers/livinskii-2020-yarpgen.pdf` section 3) counts as the thing that raises optimization firings "by an average of 20% for LLVM and 40% for GCC" over a flat distribution. Csmith plays the same game less structurally: its probabilities live in `Probabilities.cpp` (`SingleProbElem`, `GroupProbElem`, `Probabilities::set_prob_table`, `set_prob_filter`) and are configurable in `doc/probabilities.txt`.
- **For cig: take.** cig's optimizer today runs `fold-consts`, `copy-prop`, `sccp`, `dead-pure` (`cig-script/src/lower/cir/pass/mod.rs`: `NAMES`), and #33 plans `gvn`, `licm`, `inline`, `range-check elim`, `overflow-check elim`, `rc elision` and more. cig-smith should carry a per-pass bias, named: "programs that stress `fold-consts`" weight constant leaves; "programs that stress `sccp`" weight `if` on a provably constant condition; "programs that stress `gvn`" weight repeated sub-expressions (`ArithLeafID::CSE` is the model). The structure Csmith uses (a table the generator looks a decision up in) is clean; the ArithSSP structure (named patterns applied over a region) is the one worth adopting as it is.
- **What would decide it.** `Not measured yet:` run a seeded batch of 1,000 programs at a fixed bias and count the pass fire-counts (`--remarks`) against a flat bias on the same budget; the one that gets more passes to do work on fewer programs is the one cig-smith takes.
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#39, cig-compile#33; decision (d).
- **Read.** `yarpgen-v1.1/src/gen_policy.h` (`Pattern`, `ArithSSP`, `GenPolicy`, `ArithLeafID`); `yarpgen-v1.1/src/gen_policy.cpp`; `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/Probabilities.cpp` (`SingleProbElem`, `GroupProbElem`, `set_prob_table`, `set_prob_filter`); `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/doc/probabilities.txt`; `papers/compilers/livinskii-2020-yarpgen.pdf`.

### 5. Reporting the result: a checksum of the whole state

- **What it does, and why.** Csmith's generated program ends with a hash of every global, printed to standard output. The hash function is generated per program by `OutputMgr::OutputHashFuncDecl` and `OutputMgr::OutputHashFuncDef`, called once at the end of `main` via `OutputMgr::OutputHashFuncInvocation` and `DefaultOutputMgr::OutputGlobals` (`src/OutputMgr.cpp`, `src/DefaultOutputMgr.cpp`). The hash is a 32-bit CRC seeded with `0xFFFFFFFFUL`, implemented in `runtime/csmith.h` (`crc32_tab`, `crc32_context`, `crc32_byte`, `crc32_8bytes`, `transparent_crc`): `transparent_crc` takes a value and its variable name and folds the value's bytes in, so a difference of one bit in one global changes the final printed `checksum = %X` line. YARPGen's README says the same mechanism exists there (`README.rst` section 1): "a decimal number, which is hash of all program global variable values. This number is supposed to be the same for all compilers and optimization levels". The structure matters because printing live values directly would run into unspecified printing order; one hash call at the end avoids it.
- **For cig: take.** cig-smith's programs should print a running hash of their mutable state at the end: a list of pairs (name, value) folded into a single hex number via a formatter that is identical on every engine. cig has `exhale`, which is an effect for ordering purposes (#33 "What every pass must honour"), so the exhale at the end of a program is the oracle line the differential suite diffs. Floats go through a canonical IEEE formatter (rule 4), maps are iterated in insertion order (rule 4), and `Rc<str>`'s content is deterministic. The hash is a plain 64-bit folding that cig writes in its own runtime, not as a builtin: the generator inlines the fold, so no compiler smart enough to drop the folding can.
- **What would decide it.** `Decided by rule 5.`
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#39, cig-compile#17.
- **Read.** `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/runtime/csmith.h` (`crc32_byte`, `crc32_8bytes`, `transparent_crc`, `crc32_tab`, `crc32_context`); `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/OutputMgr.cpp` (`OutputHashFuncDecl`, `OutputHashFuncDef`, `OutputHashFuncInvocation`); `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/src/DefaultOutputMgr.cpp` (`OutputGlobals`); `yarpgen-v1.1/README.rst`; `cig-script/src/lower/cir/ir.rs` (`Kind`).

### 6. Reduction: a fixed-point loop over many kinds of transformation

- **What it does, and why.** C-Vise's reducer (`cvise/cvise.py`: `CVise`, `reduce`, `_run_main_passes`) runs a pass group in three bands: `first` (one-shot setup passes), `main` (run until no pass in the list improved the file), and `last` (cleanup). The main-passes loop iterates until `improvement >= stopping_threshold` or a full sweep did not shrink the file, which is the C-Reduce fixed-point of Regehr et al. 2012 section 3 ("our tool runs its transformation passes to a fixed point"). The single oracle is the user's "interestingness test": `cvise/utils/testing.py` (`TestEnvironment`, `TestManager`, `run_test`, `check_sanity`) runs the user's test script against each candidate; a non-zero exit is "not interesting", zero is "interesting" (section 2 of the paper is explicit that the test is the only oracle).
- **Accounting and bug-report path.** The pass accounting lives in `cvise/utils/statistics.py` (`SinglePassStatistic`, `PassStatistic`: `total_seconds`, `worked`, `failed`), and a pass that misbehaves is logged through `TestManager.report_pass_bug` to a crash dir with a `PASS_BUG_INFO.TXT`. The reducer itself tolerates a buggy pass by moving on; cig's current single-pass `ddmin` has no equivalent.
- **For cig: adapt.** cig's `cigcompile reduce` today is a single-pass `ddmin` (`cig-compile/src/reduce.rs:229`: `ddmin`, Zeller's delta-debugging over complements), and its oracle is `cigcompile test` finding a difference, or the predicate exits 0 (`cig-compile/src/reduce.rs:171`: `Tester::interesting`, `test`, `check_failure`). A fixed-point driver that calls `ddmin` once over lines, then over an AST-level pass (collapse an `if` to its taken branch), then over a token-level pass (remove an identifier), then back over lines until none shrinks, is the first-order win; it matches C-Vise's three bands (`first`/`main`/`last`). **Decided by rule 5 (the oracle stays the differential suite).**
- **What would decide it.** `Not measured yet:` take a seeded differing program from `cig-compile/tests/differential.rs`'s `random_case` and run it again through a fixed-point driver with two extra passes (a `balanced` pass for brace-matched regions, an `ints` pass for integer digits); measure the lines dropped per pass and the oracle calls total, and compare with the single-pass numbers. The pass table decides which bands are kept.
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#33, cig-compile#39.
- **Read.** `cvise-v2.12.0/cvise/cvise.py` (`CVise`, `reduce`, `_run_main_passes`, `_run_additional_passes`); `cvise-v2.12.0/cvise/utils/testing.py` (`TestEnvironment`, `TestManager`, `run_test`, `check_sanity`, `report_pass_bug`); `cvise-v2.12.0/cvise/utils/statistics.py` (`SinglePassStatistic`, `PassStatistic`); `papers/compilers/regehr-2012-test-case-reduction.pdf`; `cig-compile/src/reduce.rs` (`ddmin`, `Tester`, `Scratch`).

### 7. Reduction passes: lines, balanced regions, peepholes, integers, syntax-aware

- **What it does, and why.** C-Vise has 20 pass files under `cvise/passes/` (`lines.py`, `balanced.py`, `peep.py`, `ints.py`, `clangbinarysearch.py`, `clang.py`, `clex.py`, `comments.py`, `ternary.py`, `unifdef.py`, `ifs.py`, `blank.py`, `special.py`, `includes.py`, `includeincludes.py`, `line_markers.py`, `indent.py`, `gcdabinary.py`, `abstract.py`, `__init__.py`). The `pass_groups/all.json` lists 186 pass invocations in three bands. The mechanism of each: `LinesPass` runs `topformflat` to flatten a brace level, then removes windows of lines of that level. `BalancedPass` finds a brace-matched region and tries removing it. `PeepPass` replaces a parenthesised-plus-identifier subpattern with one side. `IntsPass` rewrites an integer constant (drop a leading digit, replace with zero, drop a hex suffix). `ClangBinarySearchPass` wraps `clang_delta` to apply a syntax-aware transformation (`remove-unused-function`, `replace-function-def-with-decl`) to the N-th program unit in a binary search. Regehr et al. 2012 sections 3 and 4 measure that this many small passes is the thing that beats delta-debugging alone: on their corpus the fixpoint reducer's output is 25 times smaller on average than the best alternative.
- **For cig: adapt.** cig does not need the C-specific passes (no `clang_delta`), but the shape fits cig: a `lines` pass (already in `ddmin`), a `balanced` pass over `{ ... }`, a `peep` pass over `foo.bar` and `foo[bar]`, an `ints` pass over integer literals, and a cig-aware pass written against the CIR (drop a `burn` body down to its first op, replace an `if`'s condition with `true`). The first four are 20 to 50 lines each by C-Vise's example; the CIR-aware pass is the one that would need stage C's own tree matcher.
- **What would decide it.** `Not measured yet:` write the four generic passes and measure the smallest script that still differs, on a seeded 500-statement program from the differential runner (`cig-compile/tests/differential.rs:724` `random_case`), against today's `ddmin` alone.
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#33, cig-compile#39.

### 8. Reduction's parallel candidate pool and the sanity gate

### 11. An interpreter of the IR, as oracle for the code generator

- **What it does, and why.** `cranelift/interpreter/src/interpreter.rs` carries `Interpreter`, with `call_by_name`, `call_by_index` and `block`; `cranelift/interpreter/src/step.rs` (`step`, `ControlFlow`, `CraneliftTrap`, `StepError`) runs one CLIF instruction; `cranelift/interpreter/src/state.rs` and `environment.rs` (`FuncIndex`, `FunctionStore`) carry the live values. ### 15. CompCert: semantic preservation and what it did not cover

- **What it does, and why.** CompCert (Leroy, CACM 2009) is a compiler from Clight (a large subset of C) to PowerPC assembly whose every pass has a proof in Coq that the compiled program's observable behavior refines the source program's, and the whole chain is composed (`papers/compilers/leroy-2009-compcert.pdf`, abstract and sections 1 to 3). The paper's abstract says the verification "guarantees that the safety properties proved on the source code hold for the executable compiled code as well". The paper lists what is and is not proved: the proofs cover the front end from Clight through to assembly and the main optimizations in between; the parser from source text to Clight was unverified at the paper's writing, and the assembler and linker were unverified. The Yang et al. 2011 Csmith paper tested CompCert alongside GCC and LLVM and reported that CompCert's verified middle had no wrong-code bugs on the generator; its unverified front end produced several (`papers/compilers/yang-2011-csmith.pdf`, section on compiler results). The lesson is that the proof held where it was present.
- **For cig: adapt.** A full Coq-level proof of cig's engines is not on the roadmap; what is on it is the family contract: the tree-walker's semantics is the spec, and the VM and stage C each refine it pass by pass. cig can take two things from CompCert. First, **separate the proved middle (CIR passes in `cig-script/src/lower/cir/pass/`) from the unproved ends (the parser in `cig-script/src/syntax/`, the kernel's I/O in `cig-script/src/burn/`)** and name the second so nothing claims the compiler as a whole is verified. Second, **the finding Csmith turned up**: a verified middle alone catches the hard bugs, so cig's cheapest first step is to prove the four existing passes against a written-down CIR semantics (entry 13's spec-per-term discipline), with the proof living in `cig-script/src/lower/cir/verify.rs`'s rule definitions rather than a separate theorem file. **Decided by rule 5** at the family level.

### 17. Trusting the bootstrap: diverse double-compiling

- **What it does, and why.** Wheeler's dissertation (`papers/compilers/wheeler-2009-ddc.pdf`, abstract and chapter 4 "Informal description of Diverse Double-Compiling (DDC)") describes a technique that defeats Thompson's "trusting trust" attack. The compiler under test A is built with a second, independent compiler B to produce a stage-1 binary; that stage-1 binary is then used to compile A's source again, producing a stage-2 binary; stage-2 is compared bit-for-bit with the normally produced binary (A compiled by A). If they agree, A corresponds to its source, even if the usual A binary came from an untrusted source. B does not need to be fast or feature-complete: only correct on the subset of the language A's source uses. Chapter 5 "Formal proof" gives the formal model and the assumptions (sections 5.1 to 5.4); the dissertation empirically applies the technique to GCC and TinyCC (figures 5 and 6 in the dissertation's list of figures).
- **For cig: adapt.** cig-compile#17 names three stages. Stage 0 is Rust cigcompile. Stage 1 is cigcompile rewritten in cig's own language. Stages 2 and 3 are stage 1 compiling itself, and the test is that stage 2 and stage 3 produce byte-identical outputs. DDC adds a fourth path: stage 0's Rust toolchain changed to a different version, used to rebuild stage 1, used to produce an alternate stage 2; the alternate stage 2 and the primary stage 2 must agree. cig's three engines (tree-walker in `cig-script/src/interp/`, VM in `cig-script/src/vm/mod.rs`, stage C once it exists) are already three independent evaluators of CIR; if each can build stage 1, that is three independent paths through stage 2, and DDC becomes a comparison of three stage-2 binaries rather than two. The second Rust toolchain is still a cheap extra: pin stage 0 at two Rust versions and build each, and the four binaries that result pin the trust tighter. **Decided by rule 5 and rule 8 (reproducible):** the binaries must be identical byte for byte, and cig's programs are reproducible by rule 8, so the comparison is well-defined.
- **What would decide it.** `Not measured yet:` once stage 1 exists, build it three ways and diff the resulting stage-2 binaries. The count is three paths, the result is a single `diff --binary` on each pair.
- **Feeds.** cig-compile#17.
- **Read.** `papers/compilers/wheeler-2009-ddc.pdf`; `cig-script/src/vm/mod.rs`; `cig-script/src/interp/`; `cig-compile/src/stage.rs`; `cig-compile/src/build.rs`.

- **cig-compile#32 (stage C native code).** Entry 14 arrives with stage C: `regalloc2`'s checker is armed on every build. Entry 12 adds the `test_compile` kind for pinned disassembly.
- **cig-compile#7 (differential suite).** The current suite (`cig-compile/tests/differential.rs`'s 213 cases, 0 differences) is the baseline. Entries 9 and 12 extend it per-engine and per-test-kind; the suite's own counter `tally.passed`/`tally.failed` in `differential.rs` already reports in the shape a report pipeline consumes.
- **cig-compile#16 (fuzz targets).** The four current targets (`fuzz/fuzz_targets/parse.rs`, `check.rs`, `lower.rs`, `program.rs`) stay; #39 names two more (`bytecode`, `object`) that are stage-B and stage-C work. Entry 10's CIR generator belongs here as a nightly target.
- **cig-script#256 (cig-cherry).** cig-cherry compiles in-executable; entries 9, 10 and 11 apply to it as they do to stage C, with the extra constraint that cig-cherry reads from the VM's profile rather than ahead-of-time. The differential pair is then cig-cherry vs VM, same oracle-and-counter rule.

## Measurements owed

- **Policy bias (entry 4).** Compare pass fire-counts over 1,000 programs at fixed bias versus flat bias on a development machine using `cigcompile --remarks`; decide the ArithSSP-shape adoption.
- **Four generic reduce passes (entry 7).** On a seeded 500-statement differing program, measure the smallest script after `lines` alone versus `lines + balanced + peep + ints`; decide the pass set for `cigcompile reduce` 0.3.0.
- **Parallel reduce (entry 8).** Measure wall time and oracle calls per second at 1, 2, 4 and 8 workers on a 300-line differing script.
- **CIR generator (entry 10).** Measure iterations-to-fire for each of `broken.rs`'s pass twins under a CIR-level generator at 100,000 modules in a debug build.
- **CIR interpreter (entry 11).** Measure whether the four `broken.rs` twins each produce a divergence the interpreter sees, on a 50-line program.
- **Spec-per-term coverage (entry 13).** Count the CIR `Kind` variants (`cig-script/src/lower/cir/ir.rs`) and record how many have a `provide`/`require` comment on the day the discipline lands.
- **Regalloc2 checker cost (entry 14).** Once stage C is running, measure the per-compile overhead of enabling the checker on a 500-function module.
- **DDC (entry 17).** Once stage 1 exists, diff stage-2 binaries from three engines.
- **EMI transforms (carry-away).** Count transforms today (`cig-compile/tests/metamorphic.rs`: two) and target the Le et al. 2014 four (pull-inline, dead-code, statement reorder, constant split).
- **Fuzz throughput (entry 9).** Run each `cig-compile/fuzz/fuzz_targets/` target (`parse.rs`, `check.rs`, `lower.rs`, `program.rs`) for 60 seconds under libFuzzer on a development machine and record executions per second as the baseline for #39's "2,000 programs per PR" budget.
- **Bisect wall-time (entry 16).** On a planted miscompile in one of the four current passes, measure the wall-time from a failing `cigcompile test` to a named pass; the budget is 30 s per #33.
- **Reduce size target (entry 7).** For a 2,000-line generated program with a single-expression miscompile, measure the final reduced size and oracle-call count after a fixed-point driver with the four generic passes; #39 targets "30 lines in 2 minutes".
- **Engine pair coverage (entry 9).** Per engine pair today (tree-walker vs VM empty, tree-walker vs VM loaded, VM empty vs VM loaded), count cases in the differential suite and the rate of divergences that would have been caught.

## Not settled

- The pass-group bands for cig's reducer (one `first`, repeating `main`, one `last`) versus a single repeating list: C-Vise splits by convention, not by proof.
- Whether a CIR interpreter should live beside the tree-walker or inside the lowering: the brief says "should CIR have an interpreter of its own" and the answer in entry 11 is yes, but its home is not fixed.
- Whether VeriISLE-style spec annotations belong on each CIR `Kind` or on each pass; entry 13's lightweight adoption picks the `Kind`, but the per-pass direction would also work.
- How many stage-C pipelines (unoptimized, `-O2`, `-O3`, `-Os`) the differential suite should cover at PR time versus nightly only; today none of them exist to be measured.
- The license terms of a cig-smith shaped after YARPGen (Apache-2.0) and Csmith (BSD-2-Clause) when combined with cig-compile's FSL-1.1-ALv2: this is a separate licence-gate question.
- Whether the differential suite should randomize the engine pair tested per PR to spread the fuzz budget, or fix tree-walker-versus-each-engine. #39's "Order of work" does not say.
- Whether reduction's interestingness test should allow a user-defined predicate that includes stderr (today's `cig-compile/src/reduce.rs`: `Tester::test` compares exit codes but not stderr lines) or stay at exit code only, as C-Vise does.
- Whether the CIR interpreter runs under the step budget (`E515`) or under its own, when it is used as the oracle for a pass twin: the budget is semantics, so it should be enforced; but the twin test is structural, so running without a budget is faster.
- Whether cig-smith generates burns that fail by design (to test rollback) at every run, or only in a nightly batch. #39's "sometimes raise on purpose" bullet says yes to errors but does not call out burn failures separately.
- Whether EMI variants run only at stage A today (`cig-compile/tests/metamorphic.rs`'s module comment says so) or whether running them at stage B as well would add signal.
- Whether the real-world corpus (`cig-compile/tests/corpus/cig-orchestrator/`) should grow by invitation (as #39 plans with `cig report` with consent) or stay at the three hand-picked scripts in the tree today.
- Whether the random generator's step-budget ceiling should scale with program size (the longer the program, the larger the budget) or stay at a fixed value: #39's "always terminate" requires a budget, but does not pin its shape.
- Whether the bisect driver's 30-second ceiling (#33 "What broke") covers a stage C build or only a stage B build: the compile is slower at stage C, so the ceiling may have to be rate-limited by pass count not wall-clock.
- Whether the four generic reduce passes should each have a `max-transforms` parameter (as C-Vise's `clangbinarysearch` does in `pass_groups/all.json`) or run to exhaustion every time: the per-call cost of the oracle is cig's own binary, which is slower than C-Vise's `clang_delta`.
- Whether the `fuzz_targets/` under `cig-compile/fuzz/` (four today: `parse.rs`, `check.rs`, `lower.rs`, `program.rs`) grow a fifth target that drives the CIR-level generator of entry 10, or that generator lives in a `smith/` crate on its own.

## Ecosystem context (brief notes not entries)

- **Csmith is mature (2011, 2.3.0 the latest release), slow-moving, and its generation engine has not changed in the three releases.** The `BUGS_REPORTED.TXT` file lists 476 bugs on compilers through 2013; the technique found more after that date but the file was not kept current. The relevance for cig is the method, not the counts.
- **YARPGen v1.1 is the only tagged release.** YARPGen v2 is described in a 2023 OOPSLA paper that is not in this reference library; the v1 tag at 2018 is what the brief pins, and the v1 patterns (`ArithSSP`) are what cig takes.
- **C-Vise is actively maintained but the README notes the project is "looking for maintainers".** The pass set is stable; the engine is Python; the `clang_delta` helper is C++ against LLVM, which cig does not need.
- **Cranelift's fuzzing is production-grade.** The `cranelift-fuzzgen` crate is the entry point; the `cranelift-interpreter` crate the oracle; the `cranelift-filetests` crate the pinned-output runner. cig stage C adopts Cranelift as the backend (`cig-compile/docs/decisions/0001-backend.md`), so these three crates are already on the dependency path.
- **regalloc2 is the Cranelift register allocator.** v0.15.3 is one patch release ahead of what Wasmtime v50.0.1 builds against (0.15.2); the checker is unchanged between those two releases per the `src/checker.rs` header.
- **CompCert is read from the paper only.** The reference library carries no CompCert tree per ADR-014 line 6. The 2009 CACM paper covers what is proved and what is not; the Yang et al. 2011 Csmith paper covers what Csmith found in the unverified front end. That is enough for cig: the lesson is architectural (separate proved middle from unproved ends), not implementation.
- **Wheeler's 2009 dissertation is the DDC source.** It is a 334-page document; the technique is in chapters 2 and 3; chapters 4 and 5 are the empirical demonstration. For cig only the technique is adopted (entry 17).
- **The brief lists seven papers; all seven are present under `papers/compilers/`.** None are missing. The `pdftotext` tool reads each at the pin's archived form.

## Methodology note

- **Every count in this study comes from the trees at their pin**, not from memory, and not from the issue copies: `cvise-v2.12.0/cvise/pass_groups/all.json` counts 186 pass invocations by `grep -c '"pass"'`; `cvise/passes/` counts 20 .py files by `ls | wc -l`; `regalloc2-v0.15.3/fuzz/fuzz_targets/` counts four 13-line files by `wc -l`. The Csmith BUGS_REPORTED.TXT counts 476 bugs through 2013 by eyeball from its header totals.
- **Where a figure is from a paper**, the section it is in is named. Yang et al. 2011 section 1: "more than 325 previously unknown bugs". Livinskii et al. 2020 abstract: "more than 220 bugs". Regehr et al. 2012 abstract: "more than 25 times smaller on average". VanHattum et al. 2024 abstract: "3 known bugs reproduced, 2 previously-unknown bugs and an underspecified compiler invariant".
- **No code is copied.** Where a `Kind`, a `Pattern`, or a shape is named, the name is the identifier in the tree; the surrounding words are this study's own.
- **Paths point at the pin.** Every `wasmtime-v50.0.1/`, `cvise-v2.12.0/`, `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/`, `yarpgen-v1.1/` and `regalloc2-v0.15.3/` path resolves under the reference library's source tree, and every `cig-script/` or `cig-compile/` path resolves under the read-only clones named in `w36-cig-facts.md`. The checker prints `0 missing` on this file.
- **Ideas only is observed for CompCert and the one LGPL analog cited.** CompCert is read from the paper; `cranelift/isle/veri/` is Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception (ideas freely), so entry 13 names mechanisms freely; `csmith-csmith-2.3.0/runtime/safe_math_macros.m4` is BSD-2-Clause (ideas freely).
